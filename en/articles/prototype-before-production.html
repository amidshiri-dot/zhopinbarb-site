Prototyping before production: form, fit and function

Define what a prototype must demonstrate and document the findings before production decisions.

Zhopin Barb | Published: 5 October 2026

Assign a specific purpose to the prototype

Determine whether the prototype is intended to demonstrate geometry, verify assembly or evaluate function. The purpose informs the process and material requirements.

Prototyping guidance distinguishes form, fit and function. A visual model alone does not establish the durability or operating performance of a production part.

Document the questions to be resolved

For geometry review, describe the overall dimensions and available installation space. For assembly review, identify adjacent components and installation sequence. Functional evaluation requires an agreed test purpose and acceptance criteria.

Record each finding as accepted, requiring revision or requiring further review. This creates a clear basis for the next design decision.

Consider additive manufacturing in context

3D printing is one possible prototyping route. Its suitability must be assessed against the prototype purpose and material requirements.

The crane motor fan shown in our portfolio was printed as an installation component using polypropylene with 30% fibre reinforcement. That project does not establish the suitability of the same process or material for every application.

Control the transition to production

Record approved revisions in the drawing or technical brief and identify the final version. Align the production quantity, material, finish and acceptance criteria with that version.

An inquiry should state the application, prototype purpose, available documents and requested date. Cost and schedule follow technical review.
